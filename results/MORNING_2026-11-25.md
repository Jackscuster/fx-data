## Stage 1 — item 1, gap fills

*Landing. Written here when the run finishes.*

**What is already measured and does not depend on the run:** a LONG EURCHF held
into 15 January 2015, at the engine's own sizing.

| stop | what the engine booked | the hourly rule | the bound |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.5 ATR (16.5 pips) | **−1.00 R** | −1.00 R | **−20.10 R** |
| 31 ATR | −20.67 R | **−99.95 R** | −101.44 R |

Three lines on that:

1. **At a realistic stop the hourly path cannot prove the gap, and the honest
   answer is a bound, not a number.** The de-peg happened inside one hourly bar
   (09:00 UTC: bid open 1.20083, low 1.16777) whose range covers any stop near
   the peg — so hour resolution says "price traded through it" and fills at the
   stop. The next hour opened at **1.03592**. What a tick path would have filled
   is somewhere between the stop and 1.16777: **up to 20 times the intended
   risk**, and the engine books 1.0 R.
2. **Where the gap IS resolvable the correction is enormous.** A stop below
   1.16777 breaches in the 10:00 bar instead, and the first tradable price is
   that bar's open, 1.03592. A −20.67 R loss becomes **−99.95 R**.
3. **The leverage cap is no protection against this.** That position is
   **7.3:1** — comfortably inside the 1:30 prop limit — so the cap scales it by
   1.000 and capped size equals engine size. The cap limits how much you can
   hold, not what a hole in the market does to what you are holding.

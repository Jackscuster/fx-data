## The engine fault this stage is built on top of

`nnfx1.daily_series` built every leg's mark-to-market from the FULL position size, while a `plan=2` leg realises half — a full stop-out is −0.5 + −0.5. Open mark-to-market was therefore overstated by the leg count. It **cancelled at the exit bar**, so every total reconciled to the last decimal and nothing caught it. What was wrong: `worst_dip_R` (too large), `R_per_year_per_dip` (too small) and `t_block_boot` (biased **down**, from inflated daily variance). Every figure in this file is on the fixed engine, which pre-flight guard 36 pins.

| 5 | **currency agreement**, N swept 3–6 | take the trade only if the base currency is rising against at least N of its other six partners AND the quote currency is falling against at least N of its other six, measured by **the same C1** on those pairs, **one-bar lag** |

The currency rule is not an indicator, so it occupies the C2 slot as a **rule**: the counts are computed across all 28 pairs on a union calendar — a partner with no bar that day counts as no opinion, not as agreement — then lagged one bar and written into the same two arrays the engine reads. The lag is explicit and is not left to the engine's own convention.

**Two things in that table are worth saying out loud.**


1. **No single entry asks for more than 13.69:1**, which is well inside 1:30 — so nothing is capped on its own account. Every one of the 7429 scaled entries is scaled because of what was *already open* when it arrived. The cap is a portfolio constraint here, not a per-trade one.

2. **The cap improves the result in 20 cells and worsens it in 33.** That is not a paradox: scaling an entry down scales its loss down too, and most cells lose money. A cap is a risk control, and reading an improvement in R as a benefit of the cap would be reading it backwards.

## Two faults this rerun found in its own apparatus

Both were found by checks failing, which is the checks working.


**1. The stage 3 zero-edge check compared floor-off against floor-on.** `_neutralised_with_name` set the volume slot all-True while the no-name path carried the floor and the peg guard, and the floor rides the volume slot. The tell was the direction: the *neutralised* side had **more** legs (4,568 against 4,030 on A/jack), which is the floor's own blocked entries coming back, not a `prepare` side effect. The C2 slot is the only thing that path may neutralise. Fixed; the check is re-run above.


**2. Condition 4 was restating a per-leg null with a per-entry cost.** Every two-leg label failed by a near-identical **+0.016** while both one-leg `flip_only` labels passed — and 0.016 is half of the 0.032 charged. The null's net is per **leg** (`_null_match` iterates the trade frame, which holds two rows per entry on a plan=2 variant) and it was being restated gross by adding back the summary's `cost_per_trade`, which is per **entry**. With one leg the two denominators coincide, which is why `flip_only` looked clean. A measured legs-per-entry ratio was the wrong fix — it came out at 0.41 on a long-held `flip_only` setting, because the summary counts by **entry** year while the null filters by **exit** year, so the two files are not even the same population. `_null_match` now returns the cost it actually charged, so the restatement is exact: same legs, same window, same unit, no reconstruction. **This was arithmetic, not a bias in the apparatus** — but it was not safe to assume that, which is why stage 1 was re-run rather than waved through.

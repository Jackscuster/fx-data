# Which feed the sweep runs on

Generated by `python code/l2feeds.py`. **Nothing is switched by this file** -- it measures, and the decision is Jack's.

## Recommendation: OANDA mid, from 2005-01-03

Three reasons, in order of weight.

1. **The Phase 3 proof only transfers if the bars do.** The engine was shown to reproduce TradingView on 185 of 189 entries — on OANDA mid candles. Run the sweep on Yahoo and that proof does not apply to it: the same comparison on Yahoo read 18-25%%, entirely because of the feed.
2. **Winners have to port back to TradingView**, whose FX charts are OANDA. A combination found on Yahoo has to survive a feed change before it can be traded; found on OANDA it does not.
3. **OANDA needs no repair.** Yahoo needed four: a daylight-saving date remap on 19,662 bars, 651 duplicate-week drops, 4,450 OHLC clamps and 57 bad prints. OANDA arrives with 0 bad high/low bars and 59 flat bars in the usable era across all 28 pairs.

## The cost of the switch

History. Yahoo reaches back to 1999-2003 depending on pair; OANDA's usable history starts **2005-01-03 for all 28**, because its practice feed serves close-only placeholder bars (high = low = close, volume = 1) before that. That is the price, and it is why the gauntlet windows are quarters of the ACTUAL clean history rather than of 1999-2026.

## Agreement between the two feeds

On shared dates, |close difference| / close: median 0.1200% pooled, 99th percentile 1.6229%, and 7235 bars out of 155849 differ by more than 1%. That is the size a snapshot-time difference should be — the two are measuring the same market, not disagreeing about it.

## What must not be forgotten

A feed defect does not announce itself. OANDA's placeholder bars make `sma(high) == sma(low)`, so SSL Channel confirms neither direction and the engine trades NOTHING — which a sweep reads as "no edge", not "no data". `l2parity.load_oanda` drops the leading block; any sweep loader must do the same.

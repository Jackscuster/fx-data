### Item 1 — the cap sweep measures nothing over most of its range

Jack's observation is exact for the slice he read, and the **aggregate table hid
it**: summed over all rule/book/budget/step rows the cap binds at five of the
thirteen levels, but in mode A `grade`/`allon`/`team1` at step **2011-2014** it
binds at exactly one — 29 days at cap 0.75, 0 days at eleven caps — because that
slice's worst currency exposure is **0.843**, so every cap at or above 1.00 sits
above anything the book ever reaches.

**No fixed grid can work.** The exposure a book reaches is a property of that
book — member count, netting, budget scaling. Across the slices on disk the
worst exposure runs **0.600 to 1.692**, nearly a factor of three, so one a priori
grid is necessarily too high for some slices and too low for others.

**The range is read off each slice's own distribution.** The cap binds on day
*d* exactly when that day's worst currency exposure exceeds it, so the share of
days bound at cap *c* is P(exposure > *c*) — and the cap binding on a share *q*
of days is the **(1 − q) quantile** of the exposure series. 1% of days is the
99th percentile; 50% is the median. Thirteen levels, geometrically spaced
because exposure is a positive scale quantity and a linear grid would crowd the
top of the range.

Verified on a real-shaped series before queueing it: the quantile reproduces the
target binding share to **0.03 percentage points**, and the thirteen levels span
**1.0% to 50.0%** binding.

`l2walkfwd.Book.series` now stashes the whole exposure series rather than only
its maximum — free, because `mx` was already computed. The uncapped row is kept
as the reference, because without it there is nothing to say what the cap
*costs*. Same columns as the original sweep, plus the exposure percentiles the
range was derived from.

## THE THREE FIXES

**1. A third emit tier.** `nnfxbooks.mid()` returns the dip columns without
the 2000-draw block bootstrap and without the prop simulator. The dip was
never what cost the time: `_dip95` is 400 *seeded* permutations, so the
R_per_dip it returns is bit-identical to the full tier's, not merely close.
Guard 45 asserts exactly zero and measures exactly zero. Checked again on the
whole real bank: all 552 cells agree to 1e-15 and **none of the 12 (share,
mode) winners moves**. The mid sweep is also *faster* than the full one, 4.1
minutes against 5.8.

**2. Family e is built first and written before d is attempted.** e reads the
bank and nothing else. It had no business waiting behind d's winner lookup.

**3. Fail fast.** `booknull2b.sh` writes an abort sentinel on the first
failure and dumps that run's log tail into the chain log, so a repeat costs
one run and not twenty.

## ITEM 2 — TARGET_MAP, LANDED 12:50 EST

**Three lines.** The $12,000 question does not turn on the strategy, it turns
on **how often the account may withdraw cash**. Under A14 exactly as declared
— 80% out every 7 funded days with no minimum — net dollars a year **rise
monotonically with size and never turn over**, because a payout removes profit
from the account faster than the drawdown rule can take it back, so a breach
costs only the next $549. Under a realistic monthly schedule the curve does
turn over, and the edge needed is **three to four times what this project has
measured**.

### A16 — one method decision, declared before the bank was built

The peg guard blocks an entry when a pair's lagged ATR/price sits below 0.33x
the **cross-sectional median of the traded universe**. On FX that universe is
the 28 pairs and the rule catches a pegged currency. It cannot read the 28 FX
pairs in a non-FX world — the first attempt raised
`KeyError: ('mkt','AUDCAD')`, which is **guard 43 working**, not a bug. A16
points the cross-section at the world's own traded set and says plainly that
on a heterogeneous universe of metals, energy and indices it is a weaker claim
than on FX. **Measured: it blocks 0.3% of bars against 0.7% on FX**, so
nothing here rests on it and the low admission count is not its doing.

## ITEM 4 — WEEKLY, LANDED 13:55 EST

**Three plain lines.** On daily bars the 18 declared algorithms earn a gross
edge of **+0.0174 R a trade** and pay **0.0179 R a trade** in cost — **cost is
103% of gross**. They are not losing for want of an edge; they are losing

### Why zero, and why it is not a verdict on weekly trading

Which of A2's three conditions binds:

| condition | daily passes | weekly passes |
|---|---:|---:|
| net R positive excluding its best day | 304 | 239 |
| at least 20 trades | 478 | 18 |
| **best trade under a quarter of total** | **11** | **0** |
| all three | 11 | 0 |

**The quarter rule is the binding test on both, and on weekly bars it is close
to arithmetically unsatisfiable.** With n trades and positive total R, the best
trade is at least R/n, so its share is at least 1/n — a share under 0.25
**requires more than four trades even if every one of them wins**. The median
weekly sleeve-window takes **5** trades and its median best-trade share is
**1.000**: one trade is the entire profit. **The smallest best-trade share
across all 400 profitable weekly sleeve-windows is 0.263**, just above the cut.

**The trade floor is not what blocks it** — weekly admits zero at every floor
from 2 to 40. The share threshold is what moves it: 0.25 admits 0, 0.40 admits
14, 0.60 admits 49, 0.80 admits 110.

**Reported, not acted on.** A2 is Jack's rule and 0.25 is his number. The
measurement says it is a daily-bar number and that a weekly book cannot exist
under it. **Whether item 4 has a book at all is his call.**

### One thing to hold on to about weekly

The one-bar lag is still one bar, which on weekly data means **one week**.
That is no change in the code and a large change in the strategy.

That is my fault twice over: once for emitting a sweep too thin for the rule
that reads it, and once for ordering the two families so that the cheap
independent one waited behind the one that could fail.

## THE THREE FIXES

**1. A third emit tier.** `nnfxbooks.mid()` returns the dip columns without
the 2000-draw block bootstrap and without the prop simulator. The dip was
never what cost the time: `_dip95` is 400 *seeded* permutations, so the
R_per_dip it returns is bit-identical to the full tier's, not merely close.
Guard 45 asserts exactly zero and measures exactly zero. Checked again on the
whole real bank: all 552 cells agree to 1e-15 and **none of the 12 (share,
mode) winners moves**. The mid sweep is also *faster* than the full one, 4.1
minutes against 5.8.

**2. Family e is built first and written before d is attempted.** e reads the
bank and nothing else. It had no business waiting behind d's winner lookup.

**3. Fail fast.** `booknull2b.sh` writes an abort sentinel on the first
failure and dumps that run's log tail into the chain log, so a repeat costs
one run and not twenty.

### Fault: a chain that did nothing and reported success

`booknull2b` started at 11:54:14 and reported *"DONE — 0 of 20 d-runs and 0 of
20 e-runs"* at 11:54:15 with **exit code 0**. No failures logged, no worker
logs, nothing. The single line of evidence was on its stdout:

```
xargs: command line cannot be assembled, too long
```

BSD `xargs` refuses a `-I` replacement command over 255 bytes. The fail-fast
body was about 700. The previous chain's inline body was one line and fitted,
which is why this had never shown up. The body now lives in
`code/chain/booknull2b_run.sh` and the `xargs` command line is 34 bytes.
Relaunched 11:56, three workers confirmed alive and writing. **A job that does
nothing and reports success is the worst failure mode available**, and it is
the second one today.
